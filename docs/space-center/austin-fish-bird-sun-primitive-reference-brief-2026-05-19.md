## Austin Salmon Observations

- Whole composition: two salmon form a rotating yin-yang relationship around a central focal circle/eye assembly. One salmon arcs across the upper-left to upper-center region; the second mirrors/counterbalances it in the lower-right to lower-center region.
- Side orientation: both fish read as side-profile salmon, curved into a circular chase/rest pose rather than a straight swimming posture. Heads are broad and rounded; tails and rear bodies taper back into pointed/curved forms.
- Eyes/circles: each fish has a prominent eye/focal oval near the head, plus smaller circular/oval details inside the head. The composition also has a central circular eye/roe-like focal assembly. Circles are not generic decoration here; they act as eyes, cores, and roe.
- Roe/eggs: the source SVG contains 184 visible circles classified internally as roe. They vary sharply in scale, from tiny dots to very large edge-cropped circles. The roe field wraps around the two-fish composition and creates a particle/cloud surround rather than an even grid.
- Body taper: salmon bodies are thick at head/shoulder, then narrow toward tails. The large silhouette atoms are long, curved, and asymmetrical within each fish, but the two fish together create rotational balance.
- Fins/tail: the internal metadata identifies two large fin-tail forms. In the JPG, tails/fins read as pointed crescent/trigon-like terminal structures with interior negative spaces, not as separate realistic fins.
- Shape ordering: the exact source does not reduce cleanly to one linear trigon -> crescent -> circle sequence. It uses nested body silhouettes, crescents, focal ovals, small crescent triplets, a single large trigon-like head/body wedge, and roe circles.
- Symmetry/asymmetry: the whole composition is two-fold rotational, but each fish is not bilaterally mirrored. That matters for animation: preserving the yin-yang counterbalance may be more faithful than forcing left-right anatomical symmetry.
- Internal negative space: white and pale interior channels carve through the bodies, especially along bellies/backs and inside tails. These negative spaces create flow direction and body separation; they should not be filled in casually.
- Technical decomposition highlights: 242 atoms total; 184 `egg-roe`, 10 `crescent`, 4 `circle-oval`, 2 `eye-focal-oval`, 2 `fin-tail`, 5 `formline-primary`, and 1 `trigon` by internal heuristic.

## Austin Bird / Raven Observations

- Whole composition: a black raven/bird silhouette sits in front of a large yellow sun disk, with red-orange sun rays behind it and pale sky/cloud/landscape around it.
- Head: the head is turned upward/right, with the beak pointing diagonally up. This gives the whole figure a lifting/looking direction rather than a neutral front-facing pose.
- Eye/body circle: a small circular focal mark sits near the head/throat/upper chest region in the black silhouette. It functions as the figure's key internal focal point.
- Wing/body structure: wings are wide and symmetrical, arcing upward at the tips. The body hangs below the wing span with a tapered tail/drop shape at bottom center.
- Wing details: white negative cuts and trigon-like black/white feather divisions repeat along both wings. These triangular cuts create rhythm from the center body outward to the tips.
- Sun relationship: the bird is visually anchored to the sun; the sun disk sits behind the body and wings rather than beside it. The red-orange rays remain visible above and to the sides, framing the bird.
- Directionality: the beak/head direction points up-right; the wings create a broad left-right arc; the sun rays create radial pressure outward from behind the bird. Future bird glyphs should choose which vector is primary instead of mixing all directions at equal strength.

## Practical Rules For New Internal Primitive Fish

- Do not copy Austin's exact salmon unless the task is explicitly "animate Austin's exact salmon" and source usage is cleared. New glyphs should be visibly procedural/internal sketches.
- Build a fish from a curved spine and tangent-aligned primitives, not from a rigid icon. The head leads, the tail lags, and the body should taper.
- Use circles sparingly: one eye or body-center is enough for a small glyph. Do not scatter roe-like circles around a new fish unless the scene is explicitly about roe/spawning and reviewed.
- Preserve negative-space thinking: leave openings and pale channels between body masses instead of filling every silhouette.
- Prefer a compact order such as trigon/head wedge, crescent gill or shoulder, oval/circle eye or joint, tapering crescent body marks, trigon/crescent tail. Treat that as a design hypothesis, not approved grammar.
- For fish schools, reduce detail: use 3-5 primitives per fish so the school reads as movement first. Avoid miniaturized copies of Austin's exact salmon.
- Keep fish glyphs on separate layers from water/current layers so Austin can approve water behavior without approving figure construction.

## Practical Rules For New Internal Primitive Birds

- Do not copy the raven silhouette for generic bird/flock studies. A new bird glyph should be simplified and explicitly non-Austin-authored.
- Use a strong directional head/beak vector, then support it with wing arcs. Avoid symmetrical wing flapping if the head direction says the bird is looking or lifting diagonally.
- Use one small focal circle for eye/throat/body center only if the review question needs it. Otherwise, let wing shape and direction carry the flock.
- Wing feathers can be trigon/crescent cuts along an arc, but should stay abstract. Do not imply crest, clan, Thunderbird, or specific raven identity without Austin approval.
- Keep bird glyphs visually lighter than the exact raven source when used for flocking: short arcs, small trigons, and minimal body mass.
- For cloud/sky overlays, let birds dissolve into sun-ray or cloud-current phrases rather than into fully formed icon scenes.

## Sun / Sky / Cloud Design Basis

- A sun/sky primitive scene can safely begin with circle + radial trigon/ray behavior as an internal study, because both Raven_Sun and Cosmic_Sun show that relationship.
- Use the sun as a field generator: rays can shed trigons; the central circle can pulse or seed outward phrase paths; crescents can become facial/cloud/flow arcs.
- Keep sky/cloud material lower contrast and slower than bird/fish bodies. Mist/clouds were discussed as possible but not visually specified in the screen-share images, so cloud grammar remains a question for Austin.
- If connecting sun to birds, use the Raven_Sun relationship as a compositional precedent: bird in front of sun, sun behind as field. Do not assume all birds should be sun-linked.
- If connecting sun to salmon/roe, treat "sun rays -> roe circles" as an internal morph/routing hypothesis from prior lanes, not a cultural meaning.

## Flocking / Murmuration Hypotheses

- Fish school hypothesis: represent each fish as a small curved primitive glyph with a heading, speed, and tail phase. Let the school follow optical-flow/current paths, then dissolve back into water phrases when density rises.
- Fish-to-water transition: as school cohesion drops, fish circles become current/impact anchors, tail trigons become attenuation marks, and body crescents become current bands.
- Roe-to-school transition: small circles can gather into fish headings or disperse from fish into roe/current particles. This is promising technically but needs Austin review because roe is visually specific in the salmon source.
- Bird flock hypothesis: represent each bird as a minimal wing arc plus head/beak trigon. Use flock heading, bank angle, and wing-phase offsets. Avoid detailed raven silhouettes in flock scale.
- Bird-to-cloud transition: wing crescents can soften into cloud/mist arcs; beak/head trigons can attenuate into sky-current marks; body focal circles should fade out first to avoid implying many eyes/specific beings.
- Sun-to-flock transition: sun rays can release small trigon/bird seeds that align into a flock, or a flock can pass across a sun disk as silhouettes. This is compositionally grounded in Raven_Sun but must remain a design hypothesis.
- Murmuration pacing: use compression/decompression like the water brief: dense turns at flock pivots, open spacing along glide phases, common-fate motion rather than independent jitter.
- Layering rule: keep flock glyphs, sun/sky field, and water/current phrases separate so review can accept one behavior while parking another.
