### Sketch 4 — Single carved pearl (alternative reading)
**File:** `exp5_carved_pearl_hull_2026-05-16.mp4` (18 sec)

A single 3D form whose three orthogonal silhouettes ARE each primitive in turn. A more compressed, single-object reading of the same idea. Different from Sketch 3:
- **Sketch 3** = "three forms inside a container" (your literal language)
- **Sketch 4** = "one form that becomes each shape from different angles" (our mathematical interpretation on top)

We're showing both because they answer different questions about what the pearl IS. Geometric note: we discovered that a true spherical pearl whose silhouettes ARE the three primitives is mathematically impossible — the crescent's narrow vertical band forces any single solid into a peaked/cushion form, not a sphere. So Sketch 4 looks like a curved sea-stone, not a literal pearl. Sketch 3 achieves "pearl-ness" via metaphor (sphere + contents); Sketch 4 achieves the single-solid criterion. They're complementary, not competing.

## Three framing questions for you

We have three places where our framing might be reaching beyond what you actually meant, and we want to surface them as questions rather than build them as decisions:

1. **Pearl vs spindle whorl as the container.** Public research keeps pointing us at the **spindle whorl** as the strongest culturally-grounded "circular teaching container" in Coast Salish tradition (the National Gallery of Canada describes spindle whorls as embodiments of "spirit, culture, and agency"). Pearl as a named cosmological object doesn't appear in the sources we can find. Is "pearl" the right container for what you saw, or does spindle whorl resonate more? Or something else entirely?

2. **Three vs four.** Public Coast Salish numerology emphasizes **four** (peoples, winds, elements, directions, moons, salmon cycles, seasons — per Susan Point). The three primitives operate as a generative grammar, but we don't find evidence for a triadic cosmology in the public record. Does three reflect a teaching you're carrying personally, or does four matter more to your vision?

3. **One object vs three forms in one space.** Sketch 4 is the "one object seen from three angles" reading; Sketch 3 is the "three forms living inside one space" reading. Does either resonate more? Both? Neither?
