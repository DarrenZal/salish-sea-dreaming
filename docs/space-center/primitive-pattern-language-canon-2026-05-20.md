## Core Frame

Circle, crescent, and trigon are morphology classes here, not fixed icons.
Agents should not stamp them as symbols. Each instance must have a declared
role, origin, path, boundary, and cultural status.

Use these words carefully:

- Say: "internal grammar-inspired sketch", "primitive grammar layer",
  "morphology class", "procedural primitive scene", "review question".
- Do not say: "Austin-style generation", "public Coast Salish
  interpretation", "approved cultural meaning", "traditional meaning", or
  "public-ready".

The grammar target is relational clarity: fewer shapes, better related. A still
frame should make the phrase legible before animation, texture, SDF, 3D, SD,
LoRA, or footage integration.

## Morphology Classes

### Circle / Oval

Role class: origin, pivot, impact, eddy knot, orb, soft anchor, joint, or eye
only under review.

Required controls:

- radius or aspect ratio;
- filled / outline / hollow treatment;
- role label;
- local origin or path relation;
- cultural status.

Rules:

- A circle is usually the phrase anchor: the eye lands there first, then
  crescents and trigons guide motion outward or downstream.
- An oval is a stretched circle only when elongation matters: current shear,
  body axis, eye direction, membrane pressure, or eddy stretch.
- Repeated small circles must be role-labeled as rain, foam, bubbles, nodes, or
  other low-load particles. Do not let them become unlabeled roe, eyes, stars,
  or decorative dots.

### Crescent

Role class: cupping, phase, wake, current mark, body arc, wing arc, rotation,
mist arc.

Required controls:

- arc angle;
- thickness;
- concavity direction;
- tip sharpness;
- inner offset;
- relation to origin, path, body, or boundary.

Rules:

- The concave side cups a circle, eddy, path bend, joint, membrane pressure
  point, or previous phrase state by default.
- A flow-following crescent may rotate with a current, wake, wing, body, or
  path only if it still reads as holding, following, or phasing around a local
  relation.
- Isolated moon-stamp crescents are invalid. Backwards crescents are invalid
  unless explicitly marked as review questions.

### Trigon

Role class: release, point, attenuation, ray, fin, tail, head, spray, directional
edge.

Required controls:

- curved sides;
- deliberate rear base;
- point direction;
- base attachment relation;
- attenuation or release timing.

Rules:

- A trigon is a curved pointed primitive, not a generic triangle, UI arrow,
  play-button, spike, or sharp equilateral triangle.
- The rear base should visually attach to the previous crescent, line, body, or
  membrane.
- The point direction carries downstream flow, radial release, attenuation,
  tail/head direction, or ray motion.
- Trigons at phrase endpoints should often reduce scale, opacity, or energy so
  they read as attenuation, not as a second origin.

### Line / Outline / Band

Role class: flow spine, skeleton, guide, membrane, water boundary, terrain
channel, horizon, current trace.

Rules:

- Lines are allowed when they define order, timing, tangent orientation, or
  containment.
- Outlines and bands must declare whether they are water, body, membrane,
  eddy, boundary, or construction.
- Visible outlines must not become unreviewed masks, bodies, crests, faces, or
  named beings.

## Valid Adjacency

Default internal water phrase:

```text
circle/origin -> crescent -> crescent -> trigon/release
```

Valid adjacency patterns:

| Pattern | Valid when |
|---|---|
| `circle -> crescent` | The crescent cups the circle/origin or local impact. |
| `crescent -> crescent` | The pair reads as phase, wake, ripple body, rotation, or shared cupping around one local origin. |
| `crescent -> trigon` | The trigon attaches to the crescent and releases outward/downstream without leaving a circle-sized accidental void. |
| `line/path -> phrase` | The phrase stays flow-aligned to path tangent, optical flow, river curve, fall axis, or current sheet. |
| `circle/oval -> crescent` | The anchor is explicitly an eddy, impact, joint, eye, body point, or soft anchor with the correct review status. |
| `oval/outline -> children` | Child primitives remain inside a declared body, membrane, eddy, water band, or source plane. |
| `trigon -> trigon` | Alignment, spacing, and role labels make it read as rays, attenuation, fins, feather cuts, or directional release rather than scatter. |

Every adjacency must preserve:

- a declared origin or path;
- directionality: cupping, flow, release, rotation, containment, or attenuation;
- boundary discipline;
- cultural status;
- review notes when the relation touches figures, source colors, exact-source
  geometry, public use, or high-load subjects.

Invalid by default:

- crescent faces away from its origin;
- trigon reads as a generic triangle or arrow;
- circle-sized accidental void appears between crescent and trigon;
- repeated primitives scatter without a path, origin, topology, or role;
- marks drift outside water, body, membrane, or boundary without being labeled
  foam, spray, ray spill, or review exception;
- eye-like paired circles appear without figure/eye role and
  Austin-review-needed status;
- exact Austin-source atom adjacency is reused as generic grammar.

## Motion Semantics

Motion is relationship motion. Do not animate primitive marks as generic
particles if the scene claims grammar.

### Origin

A circle or oval can be the origin, pivot, impact, eddy center, joint, or orb.
Valid motion: pulse, hold, anchored drift, impact expansion, orbit center,
hinge rotation, or path-bound translation.

### Ripple

Ripple motion starts from an origin and propagates outward:

```text
circle impact -> crescent phase -> crescent phase -> trigon attenuation
```

Use scale, spacing, opacity, and delay to make propagation visible. The trigon
marks the far edge or energy release unless Austin later gives a different
role.

### Attenuation

Attenuation means a phrase loses energy: smaller scale, lower opacity, wider
spacing, softer edge, slower timing, or downstream fade. A trigon that does not
attenuate may read as another focal point.

### Rotation

Rotation uses a circle/oval as pivot or eddy. Crescents sweep around the pivot;
trigons release from the sweep. Rotation must preserve local common fate and
should not become independent jitter.

### Flow

Flow is path-led. Use optical flow, authored splines, current fields, river
curves, fall axes, wakes, or terrain boundaries. Phrase orientation follows
local tangent and local origin. Compression happens at bends, rocks, eddies,
waterfall lips, constrictions, impact sites, and wake origins.

### Common Fate

Marks in the same water sheet, current band, school, flock, or topology field
must share motion logic: related heading, phase, speed, density, or boundary
response. Independent random drift was a repeated failure mode and should be
treated as invalid until a role explains it.

## Lane Canon

### 1. Water Dynamics / Flow-Following Grammar

Status: safest internal lead, still not public-approved.

- river bends;
- current sheets;
- kelp/current optical-flow overlays;
- waterfall descent as review question;
- mist as partial low-contrast phrase;
- black-screen additive layers for internal Resolume review.

### 5. Functional Figures Lane

Status: parked unless all criteria below are met.

Static Austin-like salmon replication is parked. Do not push static stills
closer to Austin's source composition. Do not start new figure production just
to make a better-looking fish, bird, animal, eye, wing, fin, or body.

A figure lane becomes worth continuing only when all gates are met:

1. The figure has a defined functional role in the show: swimming, schooling,
   following current, emerging from water geometry, flocking, resolving into a
   source piece, or another explicit motion capability.
2. Austin has reviewed the style direction and set the source-usage boundary.
3. The work produces a reusable rig or system, not a one-off still.
4. Track 1 procedural Austin-informed figure work remains separate from Track 2
   exact-source animation of Austin's own source vectors.
5. The output remains internal until Austin gives per-output OK.

Without all gates: park the lane and keep water/topology/prompt architecture
ahead of figures.

  waterfall stills, mist as partial phrase;

  motion simulations;

  line taper, and gap discipline;

  claim.

### Austin-Review-Needed

These may be explored internally only when clearly labeled, but require Austin
review before public use:

- any public use of circle / crescent / trigon grammar;
- fish, birds, wings, fins, ribs, eyes, joints, face structures, rays, sun/sky
  behavior, bodies, figures, or animal semantics;
- exact Austin source geometry, palette, composition, atom layout, or source
  animation;
- Austin-derived sun/ray behavior, raven/sun relationships, roe/circle fields,
  salmon morphology, or figure articulation;
- tanker/industry scenes before public use, despite being potentially
  acceptable as world-content depending on framing;
- visitor prompt pipelines and visitor-facing wording;
- relief shading, carved negative-space simulation, wrapped/cylindrical 3D, or
  James Harry sculpture-derived topology.

### Public-Blocked By Default

Blocked unless a separate explicit Austin approval path is created:
