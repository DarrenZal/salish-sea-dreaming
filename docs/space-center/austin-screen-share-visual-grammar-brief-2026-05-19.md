## Composition And Eye Direction

- Circle as focal anchor: in `06`, the eye is drawn first to the circle on the right, then sent outward/left by the crescents and trigon. In `09` and `11`, circular inset forms also operate as strong focal points on tall sculptural bodies.
- Direction comes from cupping and tapering: crescents and trigons point or cup along a path. In water examples, they should align to current tangent, waterfall descent, river bend, or ripple expansion.
- Ripple/flow sequencing should be phrase-based: circle -> crescent -> crescent -> trigon, with the trigon reading as attenuation or the far edge of the pulse unless Austin says otherwise.

- Movement should preserve common fate: primitives on the same current sheet should travel as related marks, not independently jitter. The early random primitive field in `01` is useful as a contrast because it lacks clear line-led common fate.
- Perspective should be conservative: `v004` perspective ripple plane is a useful question, but the Austin examples mostly read as flat graphic animation or sculptural reference. Promote perspective only after Austin accepts the water-plane branch.

## Practical Rules For Agent B v005/v006

### Water Surfaces

- Start every water phrase with an explicit origin: droplet impact, current knot, bend, rock contact, waterfall lip, or wake. Avoid orphan primitives.
- Use phrase order before density: circle -> crescent -> crescent -> trigon should be legible in at least some moments.
- Keep water layers sparse enough for the footage or black layer to breathe. The Austin course frames use a few marks in large fields, not dense wallpaper.
- Prefer path-following fields over random fields. Drive phrase placement from optical flow, hand-authored splines, river curves, or particle trails.
- Use line-only / additive strokes as internal sketches, but prepare an Austin question about whether filled or relief-like shapes are required for the final direction.

### Current Sheets

- Build sheets from multiple common-fate bands, each with its own phrase timing and tangent orientation.
- Compress phrases near bends/rocks/eddies; decompress along open runs.
- Let trigon scale and contrast fall off at the end of a phrase so it reads as attenuation, not a second focal point.
- Keep current sheets independently mixable in Resolume so water approval is not coupled to fish/figure approval.

### Ripple Planes

- Use flat/head-on ripple grammar as the default Austin review item.
- Treat `v004` perspective ripple plane as an explicit question: "Can the same phrase live on a tilted water plane?"
- If exploring perspective, author in plane space first, then project; do not fake perspective by uneven random scaling.
- Avoid strong 3D carving/shading until Austin confirms whether relief-like water marks are appropriate.

### Articulated Fish

- Keep all fish clips as parked or optional boundary material, not the default review lead.
- If testing fish internally, separate fish from water layers and keep opacity low for review.
- Use circles sparingly on fish until Austin answers whether circles belong on body centers, joints, eyes, or only water/current anchors.
- Let body direction come from the spine tangent and tail phase lag, but keep the primitive order visible enough for Austin to critique.
- Do not generate named beings, chiefs/specific people, Thunderbird, serpent, whale, or crest-like figures without direct Austin approval and framing.

### Topology / 3D Perspective

- Safe internal exploration: water planes, terrain-following current paths, and simple perspective projection of ripple phrases.
- Needs review before promotion: relief shading, carved negative-space simulation, wrapping trigons/crescents around cylinders, and any use of James Harry sculpture references beyond geometry questions.
- For v005/v006, separate "flat water grammar," "perspective water plane," and "3D relief/topology hypothesis" as distinct outputs so Austin can approve/reject them independently.
