## Short Recommendation

Use an architecture split:

1. **Radial / sun / flower-of-life / round-source compositions:** keep using
   cymatic, standing-wave, and wave-geometry emergence. These pieces have
   geometric centers, repeated radial relationships, and source compositions
   whose visible order already resembles wave interference.
2. **Figural animal compositions:** move first to a
   **silhouette-reveal architecture**. Use fluid/current behavior around the
   animal alpha silhouette to imply the whole body before the full authored
   artwork fades in.
3. **Hybrid figural follow-up:** after the silhouette is legible, optionally
   activate localized eddies or source-shaped apertures at internal primitive
   positions. Do not ask internal anchors to carry the first read of the body.

For Orca specifically, the recommended architecture is:

```text
fluid-around-silhouette reveal
  -> full whole-source Orca fade-in
  -> optional late internal eddies/apertures at v002 primitive anchors
```

Rationale: the v002 Orca method quantitatively hit every internal anchor, but
the result is still documented as "mixed" because the pre-reveal aperture
constellation can read as separated windows instead of as one animal emerging.
The Orca's strongest pre-reveal visual need is whole-body binding, not more
precise internal-anchor emphasis.

## Model Evaluation

### 1. Multi-Primitive Cymatic Aperture Reveal

Architecture: primitive-anchor model.

Current Orca v002 method. It uses wave-source placement to make constructive
cymatic energy near the five Orca anchor positions, then opens source-derived
aperture masks from the Orca artwork.

Strengths:

- Technically aligned. The v002 manifest reports zero source-mask alignment
  error for source-derived masks and strong local-to-global field ratios at all
  five anchors.
- Avoids generic overlays. The aperture boundaries come from source RGB/alpha
  connected components instead of redrawn circles, crescents, or triangles.
- Preserves the full source. The whole Orca fades in intact; the source is not
  recolored, fragmented, rigged, or independently animated.

Weaknesses:

- The pre-reveal read remains a constellation of separate windows. Even when
  the windows are source-derived, they can feel like holes placed over a field,
  not like a body coming into being.
- Cymatic/ripple interference does not naturally match the Orca's figural and
  biological layout. The field's causal logic is radial/wave-like while the
  animal's visual logic is body, head, fin, fluke, and direction of travel.
- Some extracted masks are compound body regions, not primitive-only shapes.
  The upper-body and central pectoral/body components are visually useful, but
  they are not clean primitive units in the same way the eye-patch ovoid is.

Expected relationship to v002 internal anchors:

- Strong. This model is designed to put features at all five internal anchor
  positions before the whole artwork appears.

Verdict for Orca:

- Technically successful but architecturally mismatched as the primary figural
  reveal. Keep as a diagnostic and possible late hybrid layer, not the first
  body-binding grammar.

### 2. Single-Anchor + Early Silhouette Reveal

Architecture: hybrid leaning silhouette-first.

One strong anchor appears first, then a faint whole-body silhouette appears
early enough to bind the reveal. The full artwork resolves later.

Strengths:

- Reduces visual fragmentation. A single eye/head anchor can give a focal
  point without asking multiple unconnected windows to explain the whole animal.
- The Raven single-anchor probe is the strongest local evidence that a clean
  focal anchor can work when the whole figure resolves around it.
- For Orca, the eye-patch/head ovoid is a strong candidate if Austin approves
  this specific output.

Weaknesses:

- Still risks a "floating eye" if the whole-body silhouette arrives too late.
- Does not test the deeper fluid/current hypothesis; it is a simpler reveal
  grammar correction, not a new motion substrate.
- If the silhouette is too explicit too early, the anchor becomes decorative
  rather than dramaturgical.

Expected relationship to v002 internal anchors:

- Early feature expected at `orca_eye_patch_head_ovoid`.
- Other internal anchors should not be expected to fire early.
- Body arc, pectoral arc, tail, and dorsal features appear primarily as the
  silhouette and full artwork become visible.

Verdict for Orca:

- Good fallback and useful control probe. It tests whether early whole-body
  binding alone fixes the mixed v002 read. It is less conceptually strong than
  fluid-around-silhouette if the goal is to make animal reveals feel biological
  and waterborne.

### 3. Fluid-Around-Silhouette Reveal

Architecture: silhouette-reveal model.

A uniform or directional current crosses the canvas. The Orca alpha silhouette
acts as an invisible obstacle. Streamlines bend around the body; flow
accumulates at leading edges; eddies form in the wake; separation happens at
trailing edges. The silhouette becomes legible as flow features build density,
then the full Orca fades into the already implied body.

Strengths:

- The visual cause matches the subject. Water current around an Orca silhouette
  may resonate with the animal's aquatic context without claiming cultural
  meaning.
- The first read is the whole body, not a set of internal windows. That directly
  addresses the v002 mixed verdict.
- Flow features have natural primitive-adjacent reads without forcing motif
  extraction:
  - streamlines can read as crescent-like curves;
  - vortex cores can read as circle-like forms;
  - pressure/convergence or separation wedges can read as trigon-like regions.
- The alpha mask can stay an invisible obstacle/control field. The source
  artwork itself remains intact and fades in uniformly later.

Weaknesses:

- It will not naturally produce early features exactly at all five internal
  primitive anchors. Most flow evidence will collect on the outer silhouette,
  leading edge, trailing edge, dorsal/tail outline, and wake.
- A physically literal simulation can become generic CFD wallpaper if too many
  streamlines, particles, or pressure colors are shown.
- Full Navier-Stokes is unnecessary and could slow iteration without improving
  the aesthetic read.

Expected relationship to v002 internal anchors:

- Outer-silhouette alignment is expected.
- Internal anchor alignment is not expected as the primary feature. The dorsal
  and tail anchors may receive nearby edge/wake features because they are close
  to the silhouette boundary. The eye and internal body/pectoral components
  should appear later with the full artwork, unless a hybrid second phase adds
  localized eddies.

Verdict for Orca:

- Recommended primary architecture. It answers the actual failure mode:
  establish the animal body first, then let the whole source fill it.

### 4. Eddies / Vortices Inside Primitive Masks

Architecture: hybrid model.

Primitive masks contain localized water motion rather than cymatic
interference. Instead of a standing-wave aperture, each source-derived region
contains a small current system: an eddy in the eye-patch, shear along a
crescent body component, or pressure release near the dorsal shape.

Strengths:

- Better biological fit than cymatic interference. Water motion inside a
  source-shaped region can feel less like a portal window and more like
  localized current.
- Reuses the v002 source-derived aperture discipline and coordinates.
- Can provide primitive-anchor payoff after the silhouette has already been
  established.

Weaknesses:

- If used before the silhouette, it inherits the same window problem as v002.
- It may still blur primitive-only masks and compound body-region masks unless
  the extraction step explicitly labels each mask class.
- Too many independent eddies can feel like separate animated stickers.

Expected relationship to v002 internal anchors:

- Strong for any chosen internal mask. This model can intentionally place
  eddies or shear at `orca_eye_patch_head_ovoid`,
  `orca_upper_body_arc`, `orca_central_pectoral_arc`,
  `orca_tail_fluke_curve`, and `orca_dorsal_inner_trigon`.
- Best used after an outer-body fluid reveal has already made the Orca legible.

Verdict for Orca:

- Recommended as a second-phase hybrid, not as the first reveal grammar.

### 5. Whole-Source Direct Portal

Architecture: whole-source reveal model.

Skip primitive pre-reveal. The full artwork appears through one strong portal,
field collapse, or flow convergence.

Strengths:

- Lowest risk of motif extraction or fragmented window reads.
- Cleanest source integrity posture: one whole authored artwork appears as one
  whole authored artwork.
- Useful as a control render if the team needs to prove that primitive
  pre-reveal is the source of the mixed read.

Weaknesses:

- Gives up most of the primitive grammar. The reveal may become a conventional
  dissolve unless the flow collapse is carefully designed.
- Does not use the v002 internal anchor work except as possible timing or
  review metadata.
- Less distinctive than a silhouette-current reveal.

Expected relationship to v002 internal anchors:

- None before the full artwork appears.
- All internal primitives appear only when the whole source appears.

Verdict for Orca:

- Good safety fallback, but not the best next R&D probe.

## Technical Feasibility

### Is 2D fluid flow around arbitrary alpha silhouettes feasible here?

Yes. The repo already has enough local tooling for a first deterministic probe:

- `scripts/figural_orca_cymatic_aperture_portal_v002.py` uses `Pillow`,
  `numpy`, and `scipy.ndimage` to load RGBA sources, extract alpha/source
  components, transform the source to canvas, and write manifest telemetry.
- `scripts/water_flow_phrase_grammar_v002.py` already generates streamline-like
  water phrase paths and primitive glyphs with deterministic numpy/Pillow code.
- `scripts/heightfield_sdf_water_glyph_v002.py` uses `cv2` and `numpy` for
  heightfield/SDF water effects.
- Existing docs confirm local availability of `cv2`, `numpy`, `scipy`,
  `skimage`, `torch`, `torchvision`, and `PIL` in the practical motion-tracking
  environment, with ffmpeg available for encodes.

The first probe does not need a production CFD solver. It needs a coherent
vector field whose paths visibly respond to the Orca alpha silhouette.

### Can it be approximated without full Navier-Stokes?

Yes, and that is the recommended first path.

Approximation options, in increasing complexity:

1. Alpha-mask obstacle plus analytic potential-flow-like deflection.
2. Particles/streamlines advected through a vector field with boundary
   repulsion from the alpha mask or its signed-distance field.
3. Curl-noise current with SDF obstacle avoidance and wake vortices behind the
   silhouette.
4. Lightweight stable-fluid or shallow-water pass only if the vector-field
   version cannot produce believable edge accumulation and wake.

The simplest useful implementation is:

```text
load Orca alpha -> place on canvas using v002 transform
build binary obstacle mask -> compute signed distance + gradient
create base current vector field
near obstacle: remove inward velocity, add tangential slip, add pressure weight
behind obstacle: seed a few wake vortices and low-density eddies
integrate streamlines/particles through field
render only sparse flow features
fade whole Orca in after silhouette is already implied
```

### Can the Orca alpha mask be used as an obstacle field?

Yes. Use the same whole-source placement as v002:

- source path: `austin-v2-ingest/approved/Animal_Water_Orca_Transparent.png`
- source dimensions: `[2522, 2215]`
- alpha bounds threshold >=128: `[283, 54, 2043, 2123]`
- source-to-canvas scale: `0.88`
- canvas top-left: `[896, 122]`
- canvas alpha bbox after placement: `[1143, 167, 2698, 1993]`

The obstacle field should be derived from the placed alpha, not from internal
color components. For a first probe:

- obstacle interior: alpha >= 128;
- soft boundary band: SDF within roughly 8 to 40 px of alpha edge;
- outward normal: gradient of signed distance;
- tangent: 90-degree rotation of the normal;
- pressure/convergence scalar: where base flow points into the obstacle normal;
- wake mask: downstream side of the silhouette relative to flow direction.

### What visual features can be extracted from the flow?

Use sparse features, not a dense field:

- **Streamlines:** paths integrated through the deflected vector field. These
  are the main silhouette-reveal strokes and may read as crescent-like curves.
- **Boundary convergence:** high density or brightness along leading edges where
  current meets the obstacle.
- **Tangential shear:** brighter arcs where flow slides around the alpha edge.
- **Wake eddies:** localized vortices downstream of the silhouette. These may
  read as circle-like or ovoid flow forms.
- **Separation wedges:** pressure or low-density triangular regions at trailing
  edges. These may read as trigon-like zones without drawing generic triangles.
- **Density accumulation map:** particle/streamline hit density around the
  silhouette, used as the opacity driver for the faint silhouette.

### Will those features align with internal primitive positions?

Mostly no, and that is acceptable for a silhouette-reveal architecture.

Fluid around an obstacle naturally aligns to the **outer silhouette**, not to
internal painted components. It may generate useful features near the dorsal
and tail areas because those anchors are close to silhouette edges. It will not
naturally discover the eye-patch ovoid or central pectoral/body component
without an explicit internal-mask phase.

Therefore:

- If the goal is early features at the five v002 internal anchors, choose a
  primitive-anchor or hybrid model.
- If the goal is early whole-body legibility, choose fluid-around-silhouette.
- For Orca, whole-body legibility is the higher priority.

## Primitive-Only vs Compound-Region Masks

Future figural methods need a hard metadata distinction:

| Mask class | Definition | Example in Orca v002 | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `primitive_only_mask` | A compact source component whose form reads as a primitive-like unit before the whole body appears | eye-patch/head ovoid; possibly tail-fluke curve | Can support internal eddy/aperture activation |
| `compound_region_mask` | A larger source component that includes body area, anatomy, or several visual roles | upper body blue-gray crescent; central pectoral/body component | Better treated as silhouette/body support, not as an independent primitive |
| `edge_or_silhouette_mask` | Outer alpha boundary, SDF band, leading/trailing edge, wake zone | placed Orca alpha outline | Primary input for fluid-around-silhouette reveal |

The v002 source-shape masks are correctly source-derived, but correctness of
origin is not the same as primitive purity. A compound body region can be
source-faithful and still fail as a pre-reveal primitive.

## Recommended First Fluid-Dynamics Probe

Probe name suggestion:

`figural_orca_silhouette_current_reveal_v001_2026-05-22`

Do not run it from this scout; this is a spec for the next render pass.

Source:

- `austin-v2-ingest/approved/Animal_Water_Orca_Transparent.png`
- Use the v002 placement transform and alpha mask.
- Do not modify the source artwork.

Render concept:

1. Black or deep-water field, no source visible.
2. Sparse current enters from lower-left to upper-right or left-to-right,
   depending on which direction best frames the breaching Orca pose.
3. The invisible Orca alpha silhouette deflects current.
4. Streamlines thicken along leading edges, slip around dorsal/body/tail
   contours, and leave a restrained wake.
5. A density/pressure map builds a faint whole-body silhouette from outside-in.
6. The full Orca fades in uniformly, filling the already implied silhouette.
7. Optional late phase: one or two internal eddies activate at the eye-patch and
   dorsal/tail positions. Do not activate all five v002 anchors on v001.

Implementation approach:

- Start at 1920x1080 or 960x540 internal field resolution for speed.
- Reuse the v002 source placement and alpha extraction logic.
- Compute an SDF from the placed alpha mask.
- Define a base velocity field `u0`.
- Project or deflect velocities near the obstacle:
  - if velocity points into the obstacle normal, reduce normal component;
  - preserve or boost tangential component;
  - add small curl-noise modulation outside the boundary band only;
  - seed wake vortices downstream using silhouette bbox and flow direction.
- Integrate 200 to 800 streamlines or particles with deterministic seeds.
- Render density, streamlines, vorticity centers, and separation zones as
  subtle water features.
- Fade in the full whole-source Orca only after a reviewer can read the body
  from flow alone.

The first probe should not attempt full Navier-Stokes, optical-flow tracking,
TouchDesigner, Blender, or 4K polish. It should answer one question: can the
Orca body be recognized before the source artwork appears?

## Expected Failure Modes

- **Silhouette too hidden:** current features look like generic water until the
  artwork fades in. Fix by increasing edge-density contrast and reducing field
  clutter.
- **Silhouette too explicit:** the alpha mask becomes an outline trace or
  stencil. Fix by using flow density and edge pressure, not a direct contour
  stroke.
- **CFD wallpaper:** too many streamlines or pressure colors overwhelm the
  source. Fix by keeping sparse, ranked features.
- **Wrong flow direction:** wake and leading-edge logic contradict the Orca pose.
  Fix by testing two directional currents before choosing the probe direction.
- **Internal-anchor disappointment:** reviewers expect the five v002 anchors to
  fire early. Fix by labeling the probe as silhouette-reveal, not
  primitive-anchor.
- **Mask class confusion:** compound body components get treated as primitive
  masks again. Fix by emitting `primitive_only_mask`, `compound_region_mask`,
  and `edge_or_silhouette_mask` in the manifest.
- **Cultural overclaim:** simulation language implies meaning. Fix by keeping
  wording technical and deferring meaning to Austin.

## Go / No-Go Criteria

Go if:

- Before the full Orca appears, a fresh reviewer can identify a single animal
  body or Orca-like silhouette from flow features alone.
- Flow features are caused by the placed Orca alpha obstacle, not hand-drawn
  decorative outlines.
- The full source fade-in feels like it fills an already present body.
- The manifest clearly states the architecture as `silhouette_reveal`.
- Any internal eddies are late-phase hybrid features, not the primary reveal.
- The source artwork remains intact, uniformly faded, and unmodified.

No-go if:

- The reveal again reads as separated portals or windows.
- The field only becomes legible after the whole Orca appears.
- Internal primitive anchors are presented as proof of whole-body emergence.
- The alpha edge is simply traced as a contour.
- The render needs full Navier-Stokes complexity before the core read works.
- The probe language suggests the simulation establishes cultural meaning.

## Bottom Line

For Orca, the best next architecture is **silhouette first, internal anchors
second**. The cymatic primitive-anchor model has done its job as a diagnostic:
it proved that source-derived internal masks and wave-source tuning can align,
but it also exposed the architectural mismatch. A fluid-around-silhouette probe
is the simplest meaningful next test because it asks the right first question
for a figural animal piece: can the body appear as one body before the authored
artwork resolves?
