## 2. Project Description
**[target: ~450–500 words — this is the "clear and compelling climate story." Trim to limit.]**

**Logline:** *We are the Salish Sea, dreaming itself awake.* An immersive AI installation in which a living bioregion, the Salish Sea, uses technology to perceive itself, so that the people who visit feel the climate crisis in their bodies, not just understand it in their heads, and leave with a concrete way to act.

*The Salish Sea Dreaming* is an immersive, real-time generative installation grounded in the ecology of the Salish Sea — the inland ocean shared by British Columbia and Washington State, at the heart of the Cascadia bioregion. Visitors walk into a darkened, ceremonial space. Marine life — herring, salmon, orca — moves across a large projection in a living watercolor language, its motion driven by real ecological data: tidal height, lunar phase, river discharge, seventy-five years of herring-spawn records. The work is built on a principle we call **co-dreaming**: it holds aspiration and grief *together*. The Salish Sea is simultaneously breathtakingly alive and catastrophically wounded, and the installation refuses to resolve that tension — because that is what dreaming does. It processes anxiety and hope at once, outside rational argument.

The climate story is specific and visceral. Forage fish like Pacific herring are a keystone of the entire marine food web — and of the climate system that web supports: the trophic cascade that feeds salmon, that feeds orca, that feeds the forest through carcass nutrients, that sequesters carbon. Forage fish are *worth more alive in the water than landed*. Markets cannot price that value; a quota system sees only the fish in the net. The installation also makes the loss audible: composer Matt Robertson translated seventy-five years of herring catch and spawn-collapse records into an evolving ten-minute musical score, so visitors hear the history of the herring rendered as sound. The silence at the end is not the end; it is where the dreaming begins.

The work is framed through **Three-Eyed Seeing** — Western science (DFO catch data back to 1888, hydrophone networks, satellite data), Indigenous knowledge (the data-sovereignty leadership of nations who challenged federal stock assessments; the Indigenomics principle that *life*, not GDP, is the primary unit of value), and the land itself as a knowledge-holder. It is designed so that **stillness is rewarded**: the longer a visitor stays present, the deeper the visual layers reveal themselves — a deliberate counter to the attention economy.

This is not a concept. It has been **built and shown twice in 2026** to live public audiences (see §5). We are now ready to bring it to the communities of the Cascadia bioregion as a touring climate experience that ends not in despair but in commitment.

## 3. Intended User Experience
**[target: ~250 words]**

A visitor approaches the installation and enters a dim, quiet, ceremonial space — closer to a meditation room than a screening. A large projection surface carries the living ocean: schools of fish in watercolor motion, a single whale slowly breathing, a murmuration dissolving back into a herring ball — a perpetual food-web cycle. The imagery is generated *live* (not a fixed video loop): species-specific generative models (StyleGAN2 via Autolume) and real-time diffusion composited in TouchDesigner, modulated by live ecological-data feeds (tide, lunar phase, river discharge), so the sea is never the same twice and responds to today's conditions. Sound surrounds them: an evolving musical score composed from seventy-five years of herring data.

**The interaction rewards attention rather than input.** Stillness deepens the image; sustained presence unlocks layers a restless visitor never sees. Where the venue supports it, visitors can also **co-dream**: through a phone-based visitor app and gesture (mudra) interaction, a visitor offers a few words — a hope, a memory, a grief for the sea — and the system weaves it into the living dreamscape and into a growing, **consent-governed archive of visitor dreams** that clusters and connects what people felt across the whole run of the show.

The experience closes by turning feeling into agency. Having felt the herring's silence, the visitor is offered a concrete next step: nearby restoration and stewardship commitments — *"here are efforts in this watershed that match your capacity"* — connecting the emotional charge of the room to real ecological action in the bioregion they just stood inside.

The measure of success is not foot traffic; it is whether someone walks out and looks at the actual water differently — a shift we capture through a short pre/post intention prompt and the consent-governed dream archive.

## 4. Audience & Impact Goals
**[target: ~300 words]**

**Primary audiences.** (1) Place-based bioregional communities across Cascadia — the watershed-scale "landscape groups" coordinated through Regenerate Cascadia's Landscape Hub Cultivator network, a Cascadia-wide organizing program spanning multiple eco-regions and ~150 regenerative organizations *(network scale per Regenerate Cascadia's program description; cited as partnership context, not as confirmed tour hosts)*. (2) The wider gathering of bioregional and climate organizers convening at the **Turtle Island Bioregional Congress (TIBC11), Sept 15–19, 2026, in Vernonia, Oregon** (~300 attendees), where the project would appear as an in-kind demonstration. (3) General public audiences at each host venue — people who would never attend a climate lecture but will sit inside a beautiful, grieving, hopeful ocean.

**Why this audience, this way.** Climate communication usually fails at the gap between *knowing* and *feeling*. Maps and graphs make connections visible, but visibility is not the same as feeling. This work is engineered for that gap: it gives the climate crisis a body, then routes the resulting emotion into commitment.

**Impact goals & how we measure them (targets).**
- **Reach** — ~8–12 community installs; an estimated 2,000+ total visitors across the tour and the TIBC11 demonstration.
- **Action conversion** — target ~15% of co-dreaming visitors accept a stewardship/restoration commitment routed to real efforts in the host bioregion (via the partner landscape-group network), logged in the consent-governed archive.
- **Felt shift** — a short pre/post intention prompt (planned; funded by the evaluation line in §6), reported as % shift; the consent-governed visitor-dream archive is the qualitative instrument that already exists. (We do not claim dwell-time sensing the proven stack does not have.)
- **Replication** — ≥1 other bioregion adopts the open technical toolkit (see §6 / Milestone 3), seeding climate-perception infrastructure beyond Cascadia.

The ambition is not a single show but a **repeatable pattern**: every bioregion developing new organs of perception for its own living systems.
