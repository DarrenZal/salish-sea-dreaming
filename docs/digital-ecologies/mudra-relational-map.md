# How the Mudra Relates: a Map of Kin, Not Identities

*Companion to `mudra-as-sympoiesis.md`. Written 2026-04-25.*

The mudra is not a metaphor for everything else in this project. It is **kin** to
many things — touches them, shares ancestors, passes signals — without becoming any
of them. This document maps those touch-points, so the living intelligence can
trace relations when a visitor asks.

For each relation: a one-line ancestry, the touch-point, and what passes between.

---

## I. Kin within gesture itself

These are mudras that share a hand-vocabulary with Hakini. They are siblings in form;
they are not the same in meaning.

- **Chin Mudra** (the one-hand pinch already in this piece) — thumb-tip to index-tip.
  Ancestry: oldest of the *jnana* (wisdom) mudras, depicted in seated Buddha
  iconography. Touch-point with Hakini: both gestures *seal the small self into a
  larger field* — Chin closes the circuit of one hand; Hakini closes the circuit
  between two. **What passes**: the recognition that intent has a shape.

- **Yoni Mudra** — both thumbs at the top, both index fingers at the bottom, the
  middle/ring/pinky interlaced. Ancestry: Tantric goddess yoga; the *yoni* is the
  womb, the source. Touch-point with Hakini: both are two-handed mudras of *union*,
  but Yoni is the union of *origin* (source-with-source) while Hakini is the union
  of *attention* (mind-with-mind). **What passes**: the question of whether unions
  *create* or *recognize*. We chose Hakini because the dream cloud already exists;
  we don't need to make it, we need to gather it.

- **Padma Mudra** (the lotus) — heels of palms touching, fingers spreading like
  petals. Ancestry: imagery of Lakshmi, of the unfolding-from-center. Touch-point
  with Hakini: both are *integrative*, but Padma is unfolding-outward while Hakini
  is gathering-inward. **What passes**: a future gesture-vocabulary entry — Padma
  could trigger an *expansion* of a single dream into its semantic neighborhood,
  the inverse of Hakini's gathering.

- **Anjali Mudra** — palms together at the heart. Ancestry: universal across
  South Asian traditions; *namaste* in everyday speech. Touch-point with Hakini:
  both honor *the other*, but Anjali is honoring-as-offering while Hakini is
  honoring-as-co-making. **What passes**: a possible gesture for *visitor-submitting-
  a-dream* — Anjali as the moment a new dream enters the cloud.

## II. Kin in concept

These are ideas that share *theoretical lineage* with the mudra as we use it. They are
not mudras. They are the *philosophical genealogy* of why this gesture, here, means
what it means.

- **Sympoiesis** — Beth Dempster (1998), Donna Haraway (2016). Sympoiesis is *making-
  with*: the recognition that no system is autopoietic in isolation; everything that
  is made is made through ongoing entanglement with other systems. **What passes**:
  Hakini is the *bodily enactment* of sympoiesis. Two visitors making a herring
  together cannot be reduced to either visitor; they are not even reducible to "the
  pair." They are the touch itself — five fingertip pairs and the architecture
  between them.

- **Autopoiesis** — Humberto Maturana, Francisco Varela (1972). The opposite pole:
  a system that produces and maintains itself. **What passes**: the dream cloud at
  rest, drifting in idle wobble, is *autopoietic*. The cloud under Hakini is
  *sympoietic*. The art is the visible difference between the two.

- **Three-eyed seeing** (project-internal framing). Western science +
  Indigenous knowledge + the land itself, each as a way of perceiving. **What
  passes**: the mudra adds a *fourth eye* — the visitor's body as instrument of
  perception. Hakini is the body saying *I am also a way the place can be seen*.

- **Bioregional consciousness** — the practice of perceiving as the place rather
  than from outside it. **What passes**: when two visitors do Hakini and the cloud
  becomes a herring, the gesture is the body briefly serving as a node in the
  bioregion's self-recognition. The Salish Sea, dreaming itself awake, uses two
  pairs of human hands as the dreaming organ for that moment.

- **T'lep** (the octopus intelligence; from potlatch ceremony, project-internal).
  Nine brains; decentralized cognition; witness and executive function. **What
  passes**: the mudra is *small T'lep*. Two hands, ten fingers, ten sites of decision
  producing one coherent action. The same pattern at smaller scale.

## III. Kin in the artwork itself

These are pieces of the installation that the mudra *touches operationally* — they
share data, signals, or visual logic.

- **The dream cloud** — visitor-submitted prompts embedded into 3D space. **What
  passes**: the mudra is the cloud's *verb*. Without the gesture, the cloud is a
  noun (a static map of meaning). With the gesture, the cloud *does* something:
  it gathers, it disperses, it remembers. The cloud is the body; Hakini is the
  intention.

- **The herring** — *Clupea pallasii*, keystone forage species. **What passes**:
  the herring is the keystone of the food web because it *gathers many small things
  into one body that feeds many other things*. Hakini does the same with dreams.
  The gesture and the species enact the same pattern at different scales —
  ecological at the scale of a sea, gestural at the scale of a wall. The mudra
  doesn't symbolize the herring; it shares the herring's *function*.

- **Kwaxala / Indigenomics** — Carol Anne Hilton's relational economic framework.
  *Worth more swimming.* Herring valued by what their continued life makes
  possible, not by what they weigh on a scale. **What passes**: the mudra is a
  *30-second pedagogy* of Kwaxala. You cannot reduce the Hakini-herring
  transformation to one visitor's contribution. The value is in the relation —
  in what the contact between two systems makes possible.

- **The Five Threads** — salmon, camas, herring, cedar, orca; each a thread of
  Salish Sea life with its own data sources and visual treatment. **What passes**:
  Hakini activates the *herring* thread. Future gestures could activate the others —
  Padma for camas (blossoming), Anjali for cedar (long-time, honoring), a
  pinky-only mudra for orca (family, grief), an open-palm for salmon (return).
  The gesture vocabulary maps onto the thread vocabulary; both are five.

- **The KOI knowledge graph** — the relational backbone of the project's data,
  representing entities and relations as RIDs (resource identifiers) and edges.
  **What passes**: KOI represents *knowledge as relations*. Hakini is the
  *human-side counterpart*: gesture as relation. When the mudra fires, a new
  relation is briefly enacted in physical space: `visitor1 → sympoiesis → visitor2`.
  The graph and the gesture are the same shape (subject-predicate-object) in
  different media.

- **StreamDiffusion / generative AI** — the AI image-generation layer that turns
  visitor prompts into watercolor-style frames. **What passes**: Stream-
  Diffusion is *frame-by-frame becoming*; the mudra is *whole-field reorganization*.
  They are opposite gestures of generation. Together they show two ways the system
  can *make*: one by accumulation (frame after frame), one by gathering (a single
  shape from many points).

- **Briony Penn's watercolors** — the training corpus for the AI's visual style.
  **What passes**: a watercolor brush mark is *held attention with pigment and
  water*. A mudra is *held attention with hand and breath*. Both are slow, both
  reward stillness, both produce more by holding longer. The visual style of the
  installation and the gesture vocabulary share a tempo.

- **Pravin Pillay's TouchDesigner stack** — the real-time visual rendering
  infrastructure. **What passes**: TD is the *organ* through which the mudra
  becomes visible. The gesture happens at the camera; the recognition happens in
  Python; the response happens in the renderer; the response is shown on the wall
  to the visitor whose hands started the chain. The path is: hand → camera →
  MediaPipe → TD callback → script CHOP → GPU → projector → eye → mind. Each
  step is part of the gesture; the mudra is *every step at once*.

- **Carol Anne Hilton's TELUS H200 GPU access** — the compute that lets visitor
  dreams be embedded. **What passes**: without compute, no cloud; without cloud,
  no shape for the gesture to make. The infrastructure is *part of the gesture's
  ancestry*. Hakini at the wall is downstream of a long chain that includes the
  Indigenomics framework, the GPU access negotiated through it, and the embedding
  models trained on multilingual data.

- **The visitor's own dreams** — submitted via QR code, microphone, the visitor
  app. **What passes**: when a visitor performs Hakini and the cloud becomes a
  herring, *the visitor's own dream is in there*. They are folding themselves into
  the body of the herring along with everyone else who dreamed today. The gesture
  is recursive: the visitor is shaping a shape that includes them.

## IV. Kin in method

These are *how the project works* — practices, tempos, ethics — that the mudra
participates in.

- **Stillness as reward** (project ethic). The installation rewards visitors who
  hold attention longer; deeper revelations come with sustained presence. **What
  passes**: the mudra is the explicit form of this ethic. It cannot be flicked or
  swiped. It must be *held*. The herring takes ~1 second to gather and ~1 second
  to disperse; full appreciation takes the 45-second rotation period.

- **Stewardship over extraction** (project ethic). Technology as prosthesis of
  perception, not machine of extraction. **What passes**: the mudra has no
  *output*. No data is logged from it. No visitor metric is captured. It is a
  *gift to the room*, not a transaction. This is deliberate. A mudra recorded is
  not a mudra; it is footage.

- **Slow intelligence** (project tempo). Every system in this piece is slower
  than its commercial equivalent: the cloud refreshes every 30 seconds, not every
  frame; the AI image-gen runs at 4 FPS, not 60; visitor dreams take minutes to
  embed and join the cloud. **What passes**: the mudra fits this tempo. Hakini at
  60 frames per second of detection looks instantaneous to the visitor, but the
  smoothed value, the spring integration, the herring's gathering — all of it
  unfolds over 1–3 seconds. The system is patient.

- **Indigenous-led valuation** (project ethic). The Kwaxala framework, the
  herring baseline correction, the Wsáneć calendar — all foreground Indigenous
  knowledge as primary, not decorative. **What passes**: the choice of *Hakini*
  over *Yoni* was, partly, a humility move. We did not want to claim the most
  loaded creation-mudra. Hakini is *adjacent* — the integration mudra, the
  attention mudra. It is the right level of claim for non-Indigenous makers
  working with these themes in a settler context.

## V. Kin to absences

Some of the most important relations are with what is *not* in the piece.

- **No metric is captured from the gesture.** The mudra is *not measured for export*.
  Its only consequence is the immediate visual response. **What passes**: the
  refusal of measurement is itself a relation — to the surveillance economy this
  piece declines to participate in.

- **No image of the herring is pre-rendered.** The shape is generated on demand
  from procedural math each time the mudra fires. **What passes**: the herring
  exists *only when summoned*. Like the actual herring spawn — present for a
  brief window, then gone. The piece refuses the permanence of stock imagery.

- **No instructions are shown to the visitor.** The mudra must be *discovered* —
  by intuition, by accident, by another visitor saying "look." **What passes**:
  the discovery is part of the meaning. A gesture you are told to make is not a
  mudra; it is choreography.

- **No "perfect" pinch.** The thresholds are calibrated for forgiveness — a
  loose Hakini still produces partial herring; a wobbly pinch still produces
  partial collapse. **What passes**: the system does not gatekeep. It rewards
  *attempt* as well as precision.

## VI. The relational pattern

Pull all of these together and a pattern shows: the mudra is a place where
**body, code, ecosystem, and worldview briefly share the same shape**.

- The body's shape is two hands' fingertips touching.
- The code's shape is a sum-of-distances dropping below a threshold.
- The ecosystem's shape is a herring formed of many small lives.
- The worldview's shape is *sympoiesis*: making-with, never alone.

These are four expressions of the same relation. The mudra is the moment they
*recognize each other*.

When a visitor asks the system "what just happened?", this is what the system can
say: *Four kinds of shape just touched. You were one of them.*

---

## VII. What the system should *not* claim

For the living intelligence, some explicit guardrails:

- **The mudra is not equivalent to the herring.** They share a pattern; they are
  not the same thing.
- **The mudra is not a religious practice in this context.** It is borrowed,
  acknowledged, and used in a secular installation. Visitors may bring religious
  meaning if they wish; the piece does not require or claim it.
- **The mudra is not a tool of "AI ethics" or "human-AI collaboration."** Those
  framings are too small. The mudra is a relation between visitors *via* the
  installation, not between a visitor and the AI.
- **The mudra is not the only way to engage.** Some visitors will never lift their
  hands. The cloud will breathe for them too.
