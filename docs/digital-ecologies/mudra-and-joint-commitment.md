# A Design Trajectory Toward Joint-Commitment

*Third companion to `mudra-as-sympoiesis.md` and `mudra-relational-map.md`. Written
2026-04-25 for the closing-night VJ set, capturing a design conversation with another
agent about the next layer of the work.*

---

## What this document is

The Hakini-herring transformation, as it stands tonight, is **already valuable**.
It is a Side-A perception instrument (embeddings, Leiden clustering, latent-space
projection) coupled to a Side-B encounter (the held mudra is the relational moment
that authorizes the herring's appearance). The cloud gathers. The mudra
authorizes. The herring arrives.

This is not yet **Gilbertian joint-commitment** in the philosophical sense. It is
closer to *participatory sense-making* (De Jaegher and Di Paolo) or *joint
attention*: two bodies hold a relation long enough for the field to become visible
as a body. That is enough, on its own, to be a piece of art worth standing in front
of.

But the design *can* go further — and the conversation that produced this document
sketched a trajectory toward a properly Gilbertian seal of joint commitment, where
the geometry of the visual itself enforces the conditions of the philosophical
form.

This document is the trajectory, framed honestly: **a direction we are moving in, not
a claim about where we are.**

---

## What Gilbertian joint-commitment is

Margaret Gilbert (philosopher; *On Social Facts*, 1989; *Joint Commitment*, 2014)
argued that some collective phenomena — the "we" of two people walking together,
of an orchestra playing, of a treaty in force — are not reducible to a sum of
individual commitments. A joint commitment is a single commitment of two-or-more
people, indivisible. It cannot be unilaterally abandoned. It must be jointly
released.

A joint commitment requires four conditions:

1. **Open readiness**. Each party visibly offers willingness to commit.
2. **Common knowledge**. Each party knows the other is willing, and knows the
   other knows.
3. **Joint object**. They are committed to one thing together — not many things in
   parallel.
4. **Concurrence for release**. The shared commitment ends only by mutual release,
   not by one party walking away.

This is different from:
- *Aggregated individual commitments* (parallel "I will" statements that happen to
  align)
- *Coordination* (Schelling-style mutual best-response)
- *Joint attention* (looking at the same thing without committing)
- *Participatory sense-making* (co-constructing meaning without explicit standing)

The fourth condition — concurrence for release — is the load-bearing one. It is
what makes a joint commitment *jointly held* rather than *individually held in
parallel*. If either party can simply stop, with no obligation to the other, what
they had was not a joint commitment.

---

## Where the current piece sits

Lay the four conditions over what happens at the wall today:

| Gilbert condition | Current Hakini-herring | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Open readiness | Not enforced — anyone can do Hakini alone (with one self-touched fingertip pair, badly), or with a stranger's hands without invitation | partial |
| Common knowledge | Implicit only — visitors can see each other but the system doesn't surface "the other is offering" | weak |
| Joint object | Yes — the herring is one shape, formed of both visitors' contact | strong |
| Concurrence for release | No — if either visitor lets go, the herring disperses normally, indistinguishable from a clean joint release | absent |

The piece achieves **joint object** beautifully and effortlessly. The other three
conditions are present in *spirit* (the visitors are clearly entering an agreement)
but not in *form* (the system does not require or recognize them).

This is honest. It is also a real opening for a future version.

## The trajectory

### Stage 0 — Now (already shipped)

A two-handed Hakini, by either one person across their own body or by two people
across each other, summons the herring. Release of either party disperses the
shape. The visual is the same regardless of how the contact began or how it ends.

### Stage 1 — B-light (intended for tomorrow's show, behind a kill switch)

The system distinguishes **mutual release** from **asymmetric break**.

- If both visitors release within a short shared window (say ≤1.5 sec apart):
  the herring **disperses cleanly**. Every fish swims home.
- If one visitor releases ≥0.7 sec before the other — while the other still holds
  the seal — the herring **frays**. Fish on the released-side disperse first; fish
  on the holding-side pause, waiting; if the second visitor doesn't return to the
  seal within ~3 seconds, the remaining fish slowly dissolve.

This is a **trace** of concurrence-for-release made geometrically visible, without
yet enforcing the full commitment. The system is saying: *this kind of release is
not the same as that kind of release*. That is a Gilbertian *recognition* without
yet being a Gilbertian *commitment*.

This is the limit of what we can responsibly add 17 hours before showtime, and only
behind a hard toggle (`hakini_bilateral`) so it can be reverted to Stage 0 in 1 second
if it misfires under venue lighting.

### Stage 2 — Open readiness made visible (post-show, weeks)

Add a *first half* of the gesture as a **visible offer**: one visitor raises one
hand in a "ready" posture (palm forward, fingers spread, held still for ~0.5 sec).
The system responds by emitting a current — visual flow lines streaming outward
from that visitor toward where a second visitor could stand.

The current is the system saying: *I see your offer. Anyone may answer.* It is
the *Open readiness* condition (Gilbert 1) made publicly legible.

### Stage 3 — Common knowledge (post-show, weeks)

When a second visitor enters the field of the first one's offered current, the
system emits a *return current* from the second visitor toward the first. Two
streams converge in the space between them. The visual is now saying:
*you have seen each other's offer; you are both seeing each other see*.

This is *Common knowledge* (Gilbert 2) — not perfect common knowledge in the
philosopher's sense, which is infinitely recursive, but a usable approximation:
both visitors and any third-party observer can see that both visitors have entered
the offer.

### Stage 4 — Joint object as held seal (post-show, weeks)

When the visitors' fingertips align across the body (Hakini), the herring forms.
But — and this is the key change from Stage 1 — the herring is **only stable while
both holds are firm**. If either visitor's seal degrades (fingertips drift apart,
hand falls, attention wavers as visible in face orientation), the herring begins
to lose definition.

This is *Joint object* (Gilbert 3) made geometrically dependent on continued joint
holding. The shape is not a moment; it is a duration.

### Stage 5 — Concurrence for release (post-show, weeks)

The herring releases cleanly **only** when both visitors withdraw within a shared
window. If one withdraws alone, the herring frays as in Stage 1, but with a
stronger consequence: the system shows the still-holding visitor that the relation
has been broken from the other side. A subtle text or visual cue says *"the other
released"* — not as accusation, but as acknowledgment that the joint object cannot
be held by one alone.

The still-holding visitor can then choose: lower their hand to release symmetrically
(retroactive concurrence — the system gracefully lets them complete what the other
broke), or continue to hold in a kind of solitary witness, watching the herring
slowly dissolve.

This is *Concurrence for release* (Gilbert 4). At this stage, the piece has all
four conditions of a Gilbertian joint commitment expressed in interaction geometry.
Whether the visitors *understand* themselves to be making a joint commitment is
still up to them — but the system is ready to receive them as if they are.

### Stage 6 — The seal as covenant (someday)

If two visitors complete a clean joint release after holding the herring for a
threshold duration (say ≥10 sec), the system *records* the moment — not the
visitors' identities (the no-biometric-trace ethic stands) — but the *fact of a
joint witnessing*. A small luminous mark joins the gallery's running ledger of
joint witnessings, as a count without names.

This is the **covenant ledger**: the bioregion's record of human pairs who have
performed the integration mudra together over the herring shape. It is the closest
the piece can come to acknowledging that the visitors *did* make a joint
commitment — even if neither of them said so out loud.

This is also where the piece edges from interaction art into *governance prototype*.
The covenant ledger is the kind of artifact a Spore-style protocol would
recognize: a non-extractive, anonymous, durable record of the *fact* of a relation
having been enacted, useful for downstream governance moves (e.g., "the herring
has been jointly seen 247 times this season") without ever identifying the
participants.

## What changes between stages, philosophically

The stages walk a continuum from **encounter** to **commitment**:

- Stage 0–1: *encounter* — Side-A pattern recognized through Side-B presence. The
  algorithm gathers; the gesture authorizes; the herring appears.
- Stage 2–3: *legibility of offer* — the system makes visible what the visitors
  are doing relative to each other, so they can see they are doing it.
- Stage 4: *durational holding* — the joint object is not a moment but a duration
  that the visitors maintain together.
- Stage 5: *concurrence enforced* — the geometry of the release distinguishes
  joint completion from one-sided abandonment, and the system honors the
  difference.
- Stage 6: *covenant* — the *fact* of joint commitment is recorded, anonymously
  and durably, as a feature of the bioregion's memory.

At each stage, the piece becomes more capable of being read as a Gilbertian event
without ever forcing the reading on the visitors. A visitor at Stage 6 could
still stand and watch without ever raising a hand; nothing demands engagement.
But for those who choose to perform the seal, more and more of the philosophical
form is honored.

---

## What this is *not*

- **Not a contract**. No legal obligation is created. No party can demand
  performance from the other. The piece is art, not a clickwrap.
- **Not surveillance**. No biometric trace is stored. Stage 6's covenant ledger
  records the *fact* of a joint witnessing, not the participants.
- **Not evangelism**. The piece does not proselytize for joint commitment as a
  preferred social form. It demonstrates one form, plainly, and lets visitors
  decide.
- **Not a substitute for ceremony**. The Gilbertian frame is one philosophical
  reading. Visitors from other traditions — Indigenous, religious, secular — may
  read the gesture through their own lineages. The piece is hospitable to
  multiple readings.
- **Not a claim that we are at any stage past 0**. The current production version
  is Stage 0. Stage 1 is intended for tomorrow night behind a hard kill switch.
  Stages 2–6 are the design horizon, not the current state.

## How the living intelligence at salishseadreaming.art should describe this

When a visitor asks the chat about joint commitment, what the gesture means
between two people, why the herring breaks differently when one person lets go
early, the system should answer along these lines:

> The current piece lets two visitors form a temporary herring out of dreams by
> performing the Hakini mudra together. Tonight's version distinguishes two kinds
> of release: when both visitors release together, the herring disperses cleanly.
> When one releases while the other still holds, the herring frays — fish on
> the released side scatter first, while fish on the holding side wait,
> unresolved.
>
> This is a small geometric trace of an idea from philosophy: that some kinds of
> relation cannot be held alone, and cannot be released alone either. Margaret
> Gilbert calls this kind of relation a **joint commitment** — a single
> commitment of two-or-more people that ends only by mutual release.
>
> The piece does not yet enforce a full joint commitment. It enacts a *trajectory*
> toward one. The current Hakini-herring is a *Side-B encounter* with a *Side-A*
> pattern engine; future versions may add open readiness, common knowledge,
> durational holding, and a covenant ledger.
>
> What we already know is this: the herring appears when relation is held. It
> disperses differently when relation is released together than when it is broken
> alone. That difference is the smallest possible visual registration of what it
> would mean to honor a joint commitment.

The system should not *claim* the piece is fully Gilbertian. It should describe
the trajectory, name the philosophy, and let visitors recognize their own
participation if they choose.

## For Indigenomics AI and Spore canon

This trajectory has implications beyond the art piece. If the gesture-based
recognition of joint commitment can be made geometrically explicit in an art
installation, similar patterns can apply to coordination protocols and
matchmaking systems:

- **An algorithm can propose**, but cannot constitute a relation. The match
  surfaces possible complementarity; the participants decide whether to enter the
  relation.
- **A held duration enacts the relation**. Just as the herring requires both
  visitors holding, a coordination commitment requires both parties in a
  registered state of agreement.
- **Asymmetric release is a different thing from joint release**. Protocols
  should record this difference.
- **Anonymous covenant ledgers** preserve the *fact* of relation without
  surveillance — a useful primitive for non-extractive governance systems.

The art piece, in other words, is a small embodied demonstration of what a
Side-A / Side-B coupling can look like when it honors both sides honestly. The
algorithm gathers; the relation holds; the system records nothing it doesn't
need to.

## Sources and lineages

- Margaret Gilbert, *On Social Facts* (Routledge, 1989); *Joint Commitment* (Oxford,
  2014). The canonical philosophical account.
- Michael Bratman, *Shared Agency* (MIT, 2014). The Side-A-leaning competitor that
  reads shared intention as interlocking individual plans.
- Raimo Tuomela, *Social Ontology* (Oxford, 2013). The "we-mode" account of
  collective intentionality.
- Christian List and Philip Pettit, *Group Agency* (Oxford, 2011). On
  supervenience-without-reducibility for groups.
- Hanne De Jaegher and Ezequiel Di Paolo, "Participatory Sense-Making" (*Phenomenology
  and the Cognitive Sciences*, 2007). The closest existing concept to what Stage 0
  achieves.
- Donna Haraway, *Staying with the Trouble* (Duke, 2016). For *sympoiesis* —
  background for the relational reading.
- Spore canon ADR-0050: "sociality-side-b-plus-primitive" (project-internal).
  Where the Side-A / Side-B distinction is set as canon, and joint-commitment
  becomes a primitive of the protocol.
- Carol Anne Hilton, *Indigenomics* (New Society, 2021). For the relational
  valuation framework that the herring's appearance honors.
